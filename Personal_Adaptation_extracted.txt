Personal Adaptation Layer for Atypical Speech Recognition A Novel Approach to Inclusive Voice Technology Researcher : Ashwin Raj Tunk Executive Summary This research addresses a critical accessibility gap in voice technology: while modern automatic speech recognition (ASR) achieves word error rates (WER) as low as 1.4% on typical speech , the same systems struggle with 18% WER on dysarthric speech —a 12.8x performance degradation that effectively excludes 250-350 million people worldwide from using voice assistants like Siri, Alexa, and Google Assistant. I propose and have implemented a Personal Adaptation Layer that combines (1) user-specific pronunciation mapping, (2) LLM-based semantic repair, (3) continual learning from corrections, and (4) adaptive interaction based on Theory of Mind principles. Initial results demonstrate WER reduction from 18% to 8.4% , representing a 53% improvement over baseline ASR, with semantic preservation scores (SemScore) reaching 0.92 compared to 0.76 for baseline systems. Key Innovation : Unlike existing approaches that attempt to improve ASR models universally, this research creates a personalized communication layer that learns each user's unique speech patterns, adapts dynamically to their needs, and prioritizes meaning preservation over word-perfect transcription—a paradigm shift that aligns with emerging research standards in accessibility (Speech Accessibility Project, Project Euphonia). 1. The Problem: Voice Technology's Accessibility Crisis 1.1 The Performance Gap Modern voice recognition systems exhibit a stark disparity in performance based on speech characteristics: Speech Type Typical WER Source Typical Speech (Baseline) 1.4% Apple HEWER Metric, 2024 Dysarthric Speech (Baseline) 18.0% Multiple studies average Severe Dysarthria 50-60%+ Google Research, 2024 Evidence : Research from Google's meaning preservation studies confirms that WER for atypical speech often exceeds 20% and can surpass 60% for certain severities, while state-of-the-art systems achieve 13.9% WER on Euphonia dataset using in-context learning—still 10x worse than performance on typical speech. 1.2 Affected Populations The impact spans multiple conditions affecting speech production: Parkinson's Disease : 10 million globally (dysarthria in 70-90% of cases) ALS (Lou Gehrig's Disease) : 500,000 active cases globally Cerebral Palsy : 17 million globally (speech impairment in 30-50%) Stroke Survivors : 80 million globally (20-30% experience speech difficulties) Down Syndrome : 6 million globally (speech delays in 95%) Total Affected Population : Estimated 250-350 million people worldwide who would benefit from accessible voice technology but are currently excluded. 1.3 Real-World Consequences The performance gap has severe practical implications: Digital Exclusion : Cannot use smart home devices, voice banking, customer service automation Independence Loss : Require caregiver assistance for basic technology interactions Social Isolation : Excluded from voice-based social platforms and communication tools Healthcare Access : Unable to use voice-based health monitoring or telemedicine effectively Employment Barriers : Cannot participate in voice-based workplace technologies Evidence : Studies show that users abandon voice assistants after 3-5 failed attempts, with dysarthric speakers experiencing failure rates 5-8x higher than typical speakers. 2. Current Approaches and Their Limitations 2.1 Industry Efforts Google Project Euphonia (2019-present): Approach : Collect large datasets of atypical speech, train specialized models Achievement : Expanded to 7 languages, collected 235,000+ samples (SAP) Limitation : Focus on data collection and model improvement , not personalization or deployment Gap : No continual learning, no user-specific adaptation, no meaning preservation focus Apple Accessibility Features (2023-present): Approach : Personal Voice synthesis, Live Speech typing Achievement : Allows users to create TTS voice from recordings Limitation : Requires 150+ phrases recorded in advance, doesn't improve ASR recognition Gap : One-time setup, no continuous improvement, no semantic understanding Voiceitt (Commercial, 2012-present): Approach : Speaker-dependent ASR training for individuals Achievement : Only commercial product targeting dysarthric speech specifically Limitation : Focuses on speaker-dependent training with proprietary databases, not adaptive interaction Gap : Static models, no LLM integration, no meaning-based metrics, expensive ($200-400/year) 2.2 Academic Research Efforts Recent Publications (2024-2025) : Personalized ASR Fine-tuning : Research achieved 13.9% WER on Euphonia using in-context learning, showing personalization value but requiring substantial per-user data. LLM Semantic Repair : Studies integrated LLMs for transcript correction, achieving 0.890 correlation with human judgments on SAP data, validating semantic repair approach. Multi-Model Integration : Research combined Whisper-Vicuna achieving 0.21 WER on TORGO dataset through context-aware error correction. 2.3 Critical Research Gaps Despite these efforts, no existing system combines : ❌ Personal pronunciation mapping that learns user-specific patterns ❌ Continual learning loops that improve from corrections over time ❌ Semantic repair integrated with personalized ASR ❌ Adaptive interaction based on user frustration and cognitive load ❌ Meaning preservation metrics (SemScore, LATTEScore) prioritized over WER ❌ Complete deployment enabling real-world daily use The Gap : Current research addresses isolated components (better models, more data, semantic metrics) but fails to integrate them into a unified, user-centered, continuously improving system that users can actually deploy in their homes. 3. Contribution 2: Continual Learning from Minimal Feedback System improves from sparse corrections without requiring extensive retraining: After 5 corrections : Vocabulary expanded, boost values adjusted After 10 corrections : Pronunciation patterns detected (e.g., compound word splitting) After 20 corrections : User-specific phonetic rules established Improvement rate : 2-3% WER reduction per 10 corrections Why This Matters : Reduces user burden compared to systems requiring 100+ training phrases upfront (like Apple Personal Voice). Contribution 3: Theory of Mind-Based Adaptive Interaction System detects user frustration and adapts interaction style: Frustration Signals: • Repeated corrections (same word 3+ times) • Rapid retries (&lt; 5 seconds between attempts) • Declining WER over session • Task failures Interaction Modes: • INTEGRATION (frustration &lt; 0.3): Minimal confirmation, autonomous • EXPLORATION (0.3-0.7): Moderate guidance, educational feedback • CONFLICT (&gt; 0.7): Explicit confirmation, step-by-step prompts, fallback options (text input, simplified commands) Research Alignment : Recent research proposed Mutual Theory of Mind for adaptive conversational agents, and studies explored fatigue-state-based dialogue mechanisms, but no one has applied these concepts to dysarthric speech assistance . Contribution 4: Meaning Preservation Over Word Accuracy System prioritizes task success and semantic understanding: Traditional Metrics : Word Error Rate (WER): Character-level matching Character Error Rate (CER): Stricter character-level Advanced Metrics (My System) : SemScore : Embedding similarity (0.92 vs 0.76 baseline) LATTEScore : LLM-judged task completion Task Success Rate : Can intended action be executed? (91.7% vs 62.5% baseline) Meaning Error Rate (MER) : Semantic unit preservation Research Context : Google Research acknowledges that individuals can benefit from ASR even with relatively high WER as long as meaning is preserved—my system operationalizes this principle. 4.
